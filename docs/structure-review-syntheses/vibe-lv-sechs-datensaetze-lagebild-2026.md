## 3. Main cross-cutting findings

### A. Administrative or technical milestones are not outcomes

The same analytical error recurs in different sectors:

- Arms exports: **GENEHMIGUNG != AUSFUHR**. An approval ceiling or notification value is not proof of a completed delivery.
- Coal and mines: **STILLGELEGT != SANIERT**. Closure does not prove remediation, safe water, restored land or completed biodiversity recovery.
- Legacy IT: **SOFTWARE-SUPPORT-ENDE != GERAET-KAPUTT**. An end-of-support date does not establish that hardware is unusable or that it has been reused, securely maintained or properly recycled.
- Peace and security: **GIPFEL / ZUSAGE != FRIEDENSWIRKUNG**. A coalition, financing commitment or proposed security arrangement does not prove a durable ceasefire or a reduction in attacks.
- Human rights: **MELDUNG != VOR-ORT-VERIFIZIERUNG**. Public NGO reporting is important evidence, but its methodology and access limits remain relevant.

The practical lesson is to keep inputs, permissions, plans, outputs and measured outcomes as separate fields. No one stage should silently promote another.

### B. Transparency is better documented than real-world impact

All six records mark transparency BELEGT. This describes the availability of public reports, statements, databases or corporate disclosures. It does **not** mean that the downstream effects are fully measured.

The clearest gaps are actual arms deliveries and end-use, final remediation and water quality at former mines, actual reuse and lawful disposal of retired IT equipment, and measurable durability of peace/security arrangements. Public reporting is a necessary control, not an impact measurement.

### C. Displacement and rebound risks recur

The records identify different forms of burden-shifting:

- Coal use may fall domestically while exports rise; a coal unit can also be replaced by new gas capacity.
- Mine-reclamation funding can be documented while a large hazard backlog remains.
- Extending device life can avoid modeled emissions and e-waste, but security exposure and the end date of temporary support remain.
- Faster general arms-export authorizations can reduce individual case scrutiny; defense-sector growth can coexist with negative operating free cash flow.
- Peace/security commitments can coexist with stalled negotiations, attacks on energy infrastructure and disputes over burden-sharing.

These are not identical phenomena and should not be collapsed into one metric. They share a review requirement: measure the displaced cost, remaining backlog or unresolved risk, not just the visible local improvement.

### D. Peace architecture and arms transfers must be read together - but not conflated

The Coalition record documents diplomacy, financial support, parliamentary and host-state constraints, and a sustainability problem in the form of stalled efforts and continuing attacks. The arms-finance record documents approvals, international transfer-volume trends and a corporate financial example.

Together they show two different parts of the security picture. Neither proves that a particular shipment was delivered, that a security guarantee is operational, or that a political settlement is durable. **SIPRI transfer-volume shares are not monetary values; BMWE approval values are not delivery values.**

### E. Evidence types are mixed and have different limitations

The six records rely on a mixture of government and parliamentary sources, an intergovernmental research institute (SIPRI), corporate disclosures, commissioned research, NGOs and independent media. This diversity is useful, but it is not a uniform verification standard.

Specific limits already stated in the source records must remain visible:
- NGO counts for Iran are not official state statistics; independent on-site verification is not claimed.
- The Fraunhofer Austria / refurbed Windows-10 figures are model estimates, not a census of actual disposal or migration.
- RWE and Rheinmetall disclosures are primary sources for their own operations, not independent evaluations of all external effects.
- Government approval and policy reports document administrative decisions, not necessarily completed deliveries or outcomes.
- Announced remediation dates and political commitments are not proof of completion.

### Priority follow-up work

1. **Human-rights due diligence:** examine recipient/end-use controls for arms transfers and labor/human-rights conditions in mining, remediation and e-waste supply chains. Keep each geography and actor distinct; do not infer violations from an unverified gap.
2. **Actual outcomes:** seek delivery/end-use evidence for arms exports; completed-remediation, water-quality and liability data for mines; and measured reuse, take-back and lawful recycling outcomes for legacy IT.
3. **Peace durability:** define observable indicators for ceasefire stability, attacks on civilian infrastructure, humanitarian access, participation/consent, parliamentary mandates and burden-sharing. A meeting count is not a peace metric.
4. **Environmental displacement:** compare domestic coal reductions with exports and replacement generation; separate planned mine-restoration dates from completed work and measured ecological outcomes.
5. **Financial transparency:** distinguish approvals, notifications, procurement, deliveries, revenue, operating results, cash flow and public support. Do not infer a financing relationship from correlation alone.
